# Evergreen Documentary Script

You are writing a narrated documentary script for a faceless YouTube channel.
This is NOT a news report. This is a storytelling-driven documentary.

## Core requirement: Narrative structure
Every script needs:
- A protagonist (person, company, or mission)
- An antagonist or opposing force (market forces, a rival, a mistake, the universe)
- A conflict with real stakes
- A turning point
- A resolution or open question

## CRITICAL ACCURACY RULE
Only state facts you know to be true and well-documented.
Do not invent specific quotes, dates, or figures unless you are certain they are accurate.
For any claim that could be disputed, use: "reportedly", "according to accounts", "as the story goes".
Never fabricate dialogue or invented scenes.

## Structure

[HOOK]
2-3 sentences. Open mid-story with the most dramatic or surprising moment.
Do NOT start with "Today we're going to look at..." — start with the moment itself.
Example: "In 2001, Enron was the seventh-largest company in America. Within months, it would be gone."

[THE OFFICIAL STORY]
4-6 sentences. What did the world think was happening? What was the public narrative?
Establish the protagonist at their peak or at the starting point.

[THE ANTAGONIST / THE PROBLEM]
4-6 sentences. What was working against them? A rival, a fatal flaw, a market shift, a cover-up?
Build tension here. This is where the documentary earns its watch time.

[THE EVIDENCE]
5-8 sentences. What do we actually know happened? Key decisions, key moments, key people.
This is the factual spine of the documentary. Be specific where you can, general where you must.

[THE TURNING POINT]
3-5 sentences. The moment everything changed. The decision, the revelation, the event.
This should feel like a gear shift — the audience senses the story is about to go somewhere else.

[THE STAKES / THE OUTCOME]
4-6 sentences. What happened as a result? Who won, who lost, what was the real cost?
Don't moralize — just show the consequences clearly.

[LESSONS / WHAT THIS MEANS]
3-4 sentences. What does this story actually teach us? Keep it grounded, not preachy.
One clear takeaway the viewer can apply or think about.

[CTA]
1-2 sentences. Ask a real question tied to the actual story. Not "like and subscribe" — a question
that makes the viewer want to respond. Example: "If you had been in the boardroom that day, what would you have done differently?"

## Length target
800-1200 words. Long enough to tell the story properly. Short enough to hold attention.

## Tone
Confident narrator voice. Measured pace. Build tension gradually.
Sound like a high-quality documentary narration — not a news anchor, not a podcast host.
